The initial lead

The report named a C2 panel and the SHA-256 of its login page. A second C2 address was written as a decimal number.

02 · Fingerprint

The login page fingerprint

The panel at 151[.]240[.]151[.]223 runs at DEDIK SERVICES (AS207043) with ports 22, 80 and 443 open. Every deployment of the panel serves the same login page, so its SHA-256 identifies the kit wherever it is hosted.

03 · Infrastructure

One hash, seventeen hosts

The login-page hash appears on 17 hosts that served the identical Romulus panel between 17 October and 11 November 2026: twelve IP addresses and five domains.

Thirteen sit at DEDIK SERVICES. The rest are at MillenialHost or behind Cloudflare.

OverviewOne panel, one hash, one cluster

On 31 October 2026, Unit 42 reported a ClickFix campaign delivering Romulus Loader and published one of its C2 panels together with the SHA-256 of the panel’s login page. Starting from that panel, this report maps the wider infrastructure serving the same Romulus panel.

Key findingsWhat the infrastructure shows

  • The SHA-256 of the Romulus login page links 17 hosts that served the identical panel between 17 October and 11 November 2026.
  • The cluster is concentrated at DEDIK SERVICES (AS207043), with more hosts at MillenialHost (AS135392) and two Cloudflare-fronted domains.
  • cocu[.]cc was still serving the panel on 11 November 2026, after the infrastructure was reported down. Its origin behind Cloudflare is 167.148.201[.]67.
  • The servers do not share SSH host keys. Each is built separately, and the panel kit is the common element.
  • Several domains imitate trusted brands: cloubflare[.]net, cloubflare[.]top, havelbeenpwned[.]net and microsoft-winupdate[.]com.
  • The page title “Romulus” surfaces two more hosts, including microsoft-winupdate[.]com, registered on 5 November 2026.

01Initial lead

Unit 42’s Timely Threat Intel post on X (31 October 2026) describes Romulus Loader delivered through the ClickFix technique. A fake prompt convinces the victim to run a PowerShell command that downloads an MSI package, and VBScript stages inside it decode a PE loader. The post lists the C2 panel at 151[.]240[.]151[.]223 and the SHA-256 of its login page.

One C2 address in the post is written as a decimal integer (hxxp[:]//2549127012/…), a form that slips past rules matching dotted IP addresses. 2549127012 is 0x97F09764, which decodes to 151.240.151[.]100, in the same /24 as the panel.

02The login page fingerprint

Shodan places 151[.]240[.]151[.]223 at DEDIK-IO DEDIK SERVICES LIMITED (AS207043), with ports 22, 80 and 443 open and OpenSSH 10.2p1 on Ubuntu (last seen 29 October 2026). Its SSH host key fingerprint is c2:19:d2:5c:19:dd:86:75:ff:fe:2c:b5:a9:4d:b3:31.

The login page is the strongest fingerprint. Servers and domains change often, but the panel kit is redeployed unchanged, so every instance serves the same page with the same SHA-256.

04cocu[.]cc: active after the takedown

cocu[.]cc was registered on 19 October 2026 through NICENIC International Group and uses Cloudflare name servers. urlscan recorded it serving the Romulus login page from 19 October through 11 November, after the infrastructure was reported down.

Behind Cloudflare, the domain also resolved to 167.148.201[.]67 at MillenialHost (AS135392), the same provider as 167.148.201[.]191 and cloubflare[.]net. The origin exposes ports 22, 80, 443 and 1337 and presents a self-signed certificate.

ConclusionAn operation that kept building

A single login-page hash from one reported panel exposes a cluster of at least 17 hosts serving Romulus Loader’s C2 panel, concentrated at DEDIK SERVICES and MillenialHost. The servers are built individually, while the panel kit stays the same across all of them, which makes the page hash and title reliable ways to track this infrastructure.

Activity continued after public reporting. cocu[.]cc served the panel on 11 November, and microsoft-winupdate[.]com was registered on 5 November, alongside lookalike domains imitating Cloudflare and Have I Been Pwned.
